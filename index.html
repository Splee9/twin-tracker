Digital Twin

A calibrated, continuously re-fit model of one endurance athlete: how training converts into fitness (a Banister impulse-response model on eight seasons of runs and rides) and which signals move recovery (a ridge model on ~700 nights). Numpy only, no fine-tuned LLM. Everything on this page is a model output; no raw physiology, nutrition or lifestyle row leaves the machine.

The headline forecast is the efficiency engine, not the load model. The walk-forward backtest below says so, and reporting the more sophisticated model instead of the more accurate one is the mistake the eval exists to catch.

Observed vs fitted pseudo-tests — weekly means

Three label types on one VDOT-equivalent scale: race results, threshold pace at a fixed heart rate, and flat easy-run efficiency. Dots are weekly means of observations; the line is the model.

Model selection — PRESS over the time-constant grid

Leave-one-out prediction error per observation for every (τfitness, τfatigue) pair. A flat valley means the taus are not identified by these labels; the fixed 42/7 prior is shown beside the free fit.

Walk-forward backtest by build

Every engine is fit strictly on data before a build starts, then scored on that build's pseudo-tests and its race. Race error in minutes at marathon distance.

Recovery — what moves tomorrow's HRV, RHR and sleep score

Standardized ridge coefficients (target in z-units per one SD of the feature) with 80% bootstrap intervals, rolling-origin cross-validated. Skill is the RMSE improvement over "tomorrow = today".

Check-in adherence

Days per week the athlete answered the one-line Discord check-in; lifestyle covariates enter the recovery model once sixty answered days exist.
